07 / Know what you’re seeing

Your sleep is personal.

Apple Health is optional. On your phone, you choose what the app may read or write. Your profile should contain your own records, never Luca’s.

“Time in bed” is not always “time asleep.”

The Pod measures signals in the mattress. Apple Watch is a separate source. Estimates and missing periods should be labeled; an unexpectedly short night is a reason to check the data, not assume you only slept that long.

What if the app disconnects?

Check Wi-Fi or your private remote connection, then reconnect. The Pod stores its routines locally, so closing the app does not normally cancel them. A disconnected screen cannot confirm a new command was received.

What if I feel too hot or cold?

Use Temp to adjust your own target or turn your side off. Wait a few minutes for a small change. If something seems wrong, stop using the controls and ask Luca to check the system.

Can I move the bed frame?

Only if the base controls are available and tested. The frame can move the whole bed, including the other person. Check with them first and keep Stop within reach.

Will my data go to an AI service?

Cloud analysis should require your explicit choice. A local report and a report sent to a cloud provider are different options. Check the sharing description before enabling a provider.

Can the bed tell which person is lying there?

It detects presence, not someone’s identity. After the shared setup, your assigned side helps associate records with you. If you swap sides or sleep in the middle, tell the app; uncertain periods should not be treated as reliable personal sleep data.
